LESSON 24

Practical SEO Audit Project

Welcome to your final lesson, bro! In this project, you'll apply your SEO knowledge to inspect a website, identify problems, and prepare an actionable SEO audit report.

1. What Is an SEO Audit?

Simple definition: An SEO audit is a systematic examination of a website to identify issues that may affect its visibility in search engines.

Friend explanation: Imagine a client asks you, "Why is my website not getting traffic from Google?" Instead of guessing, you check the website's technical setup, content, keywords, mobile experience, and performance. Aa complete checking process ni SEO audit antaru.

2. Project Objective

For this practice project, imagine you're auditing a website for a local bakery.

Sample website: A bakery website with these pages:

  • Homepage
  • Birthday Cakes
  • Wedding Cakes
  • Chocolate Cakes
  • Contact Us

These pages are fictional examples for practice. Replace them with pages from your own website when performing a real audit.

3. Step 1 — Check Technical SEO

First, verify whether search engines can access important pages.

  • Check whether important URLs return the expected HTTP status codes.
  • Identify broken links and unnecessary redirect chains.
  • Review the robots.txt file.
  • Check whether an XML sitemap exists and contains appropriate URLs.
  • Look for accidental noindex directives.
  • Check canonical tags on important pages.
  • Verify that HTTPS is configured correctly.

Example Finding

Suppose the birthday cakes page returns HTTP 404, even though the page should exist.

Problem: Visitors and search engines cannot access the intended page at that URL.

Recommended action: Check the URL, restore the page if appropriate, or redirect it to a relevant replacement if one exists.

4. Step 2 — Check Keyword Targeting

Check whether each important page targets a relevant search topic and matches the likely search intent.

Page Example Target Keyword Search Intent
Homepage Bakery near me Local / Transactional
Birthday Cakes Birthday cakes Transactional
Wedding Cakes Custom wedding cakes Transactional
Chocolate Cakes Chocolate cake varieties Commercial / Informational

These are starting ideas, not validated keyword recommendations. Use keyword research to confirm search demand, competition, and intent.

5. Step 3 — Check On-Page SEO

Inspect each important page for the following:

  • A unique and descriptive title tag.
  • A useful meta description.
  • A clear main heading.
  • Logical subheadings and readable content.
  • Relevant keywords used naturally.
  • Descriptive image alt text when appropriate.
  • Helpful internal links to related pages.
  • Clear information that satisfies the visitor's needs.

Example

Weak title: Home

Improved example: Fresh Cakes and Custom Orders | Bakery Name

The improved title communicates the page's topic more clearly. Choose wording that accurately reflects the real page and business.

6. Step 4 — Check Website Performance

Review how quickly the website loads and how smoothly users can interact with it.

  • Check loading performance on mobile and desktop.
  • Look for oversized images.
  • Identify unnecessary JavaScript and CSS.
  • Check layout shifts during loading.
  • Investigate slow server responses.
  • Review Core Web Vitals when reliable field data is available.

Remember the Core Web Vitals:

  • LCP: Measures loading performance.
  • INP: Measures responsiveness to user interactions.
  • CLS: Measures unexpected visual layout shifts.

Don't guess whether a website is fast or slow. Measure it and investigate the results.

7. Step 5 — Check Mobile SEO

Test the website on a mobile-sized screen.

  • Check whether the layout fits the screen.
  • Verify that text is readable without unnecessary zooming.
  • Make sure buttons and links are easy to tap.
  • Check whether important content is available on mobile.
  • Test forms, menus, and contact options.
  • Check whether mobile pages have technical errors.

8. Step 6 — Check Indexing and Search Performance

Use Google Search Console, when available, to investigate important URLs and understand how they perform in Google Search.

  • Inspect important page URLs.
  • Review indexing reports and investigate unexpected exclusions.
  • Check whether the sitemap has been processed.
  • Review clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position.
  • Find pages with declining search performance.
  • Identify queries that could reveal content opportunities.

A newly published page may need time to be crawled and indexed. Sitemap submission and indexing requests do not guarantee inclusion in Google Search.

9. Step 7 — Check Local SEO

For a local bakery or other local business, review:

  • Whether its Google Business Profile exists and is accurate.
  • Whether its business name, address, and phone number are consistent.
  • Whether business hours and service information are correct.
  • Whether the website provides useful location information.
  • Whether customers can easily contact the business.
  • Whether genuine customer reviews are managed appropriately.

10. Step 8 — Create an SEO Audit Report

An audit should not merely list problems. It should explain their impact and recommend what to do next.

Use this sample format:

Issue Potential Impact Recommended Action Priority
Important page returns 404 Page unavailable to users Restore the page or redirect to a relevant replacement High
Duplicate title tags Pages may be less clearly differentiated Review and write descriptive, page-specific titles Medium
Oversized images May slow down loading Resize and compress images appropriately Medium
Missing internal links Users may struggle to discover related content Add useful links between relevant pages Medium
Incorrect business hours Customers may receive inaccurate information Update business information wherever it is published High

These are example findings only. Confirm each issue on the actual website before including it in a real report.

11. Step 9 — Prioritize Your Findings

Not every SEO issue needs to be fixed immediately. Prioritize based on impact, urgency, effort, and the evidence you have.

  • High priority: Important pages are inaccessible, or critical indexing problems are confirmed.
  • Medium priority: Meaningful content, internal linking, or performance improvements are needed.
  • Low priority: Minor improvements with limited expected impact.

12. Your Final Practical Assignment

Now it's your turn! Choose a website that you own or have permission to audit.

  1. List at least five important website URLs.
  2. Check the HTTP status of each URL.
  3. Review title tags, meta descriptions, and heading structure.
  4. Check internal links and image alt text.
  5. Inspect robots.txt, the XML sitemap, and canonical tags.
  6. Measure loading performance on mobile and desktop.
  7. Review indexing and search performance if Search Console data is available.
  8. Identify at least five verified issues or improvement opportunities.
  9. Assign priorities and recommend practical fixes.
  10. Prepare a report describing your findings and next steps.

13. Final SEO Audit Checklist

  • ☐ Technical SEO checked
  • ☐ HTTP status codes verified
  • ☐ Robots.txt and sitemap reviewed
  • ☐ Canonical tags checked
  • ☐ Keyword targeting reviewed
  • ☐ Title tags and meta descriptions reviewed
  • ☐ Headings and content checked
  • ☐ Internal links reviewed
  • ☐ Image optimization checked
  • ☐ Mobile usability reviewed
  • ☐ Website performance measured
  • ☐ Search Console data reviewed if available
  • ☐ Local SEO checked when relevant
  • ☐ Findings prioritized and documented
