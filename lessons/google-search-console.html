LESSON 22

Google Search Console

Google Search Console (GSC) is a free tool that helps website owners understand how their websites appear in Google Search and identify certain technical SEO issues.

1. What Is Google Search Console?

Google Search Console helps you monitor your website's search performance, indexing status, and eligible search enhancements.

Simple definition: It helps you understand how Google sees your website in search results.

Friend explanation: Bro, imagine you built a website. You want to know whether Google has discovered your pages, how many people see them in search, and whether any pages have indexing problems. GSC helps you investigate these things.

2. Why Do We Need Google Search Console?

  • Understand how users discover your website through Google Search.
  • Check search impressions, clicks, and average position.
  • Inspect individual URLs and their indexing status.
  • Submit an XML sitemap for Google to discover URLs.
  • Identify certain indexing and crawling issues.
  • Monitor eligible rich-result and Core Web Vitals reports when data is available.

3. Important Google Search Console Reports

Performance Report

This report helps you understand how your website performs in Google Search.

  • Total clicks: How many times users clicked your website in Google Search.
  • Total impressions: How many times your website appeared in search results.
  • Average CTR: The percentage of impressions that resulted in clicks.
  • Average position: The average position of your search result across the measured data.

Example

Suppose your website appears in Google Search 1,000 times and receives 50 clicks.

CTR = (Clicks / Impressions) × 100

CTR = (50 / 1,000) × 100 = 5%

This means 5% of the recorded impressions resulted in clicks.

Indexing Report

The indexing report helps you understand which pages Google has indexed and which pages have not been indexed.

Possible reasons for pages not being indexed include:

  • The page has a noindex directive.
  • The page is blocked from crawling by robots.txt.
  • The page redirects to another URL.
  • The page returns an error, such as HTTP 404.
  • Google has discovered the page but has not crawled it yet.

Important: A page not being indexed does not always mean something is broken. Investigate the reason before making changes.

URL Inspection Tool

URL Inspection lets you examine a specific URL and see information about Google's indexing of that page.

For example, if you publish a new page, you can inspect its URL to investigate whether Google has indexed it.

You may also be able to request indexing, but this does not guarantee that Google will index the page immediately or at all.

Sitemaps Report

This report lets you submit and monitor your XML sitemap. A sitemap helps Google discover URLs, but submitting one does not guarantee indexing.

Core Web Vitals Report

When sufficient field data is available, this report provides information about real-user loading performance, responsiveness, and visual stability.

4. How to Set Up Google Search Console

  1. Open Google Search Console.
  2. Add your website as a property.
  3. Choose a Domain property or a URL-prefix property.
  4. Verify that you own or control the website.
  5. After verification, explore the available reports.
  6. Submit your XML sitemap if you have one.
  7. Inspect important URLs and investigate any relevant issues.

Domain property: Covers the domain's protocols and subdomains. Verification generally requires a DNS record.

URL-prefix property: Covers URLs that begin with the specified prefix and supports several verification methods.

5. Real-Time Example

Imagine you own a website for a local bakery.

  1. You publish a page about birthday cakes.
  2. You check the page using URL Inspection.
  3. You investigate any indexing issues shown by Google Search Console.
  4. After the page starts appearing in search, you monitor its impressions and clicks.
  5. You identify search queries that bring visitors to the page.
  6. You improve the page's content and title when the evidence suggests an opportunity.

This is how GSC helps you make SEO decisions based on actual search data instead of guessing.

6. Google Search Console vs Google Analytics

  • Google Search Console: Focuses on Google Search visibility, search performance, and indexing information.
  • Google Analytics: Focuses on what users do after they visit your website, such as engagement and conversions.

Simple ga cheppalante, GSC helps you understand how users find your website through Google Search. Analytics helps you understand what users do after visiting your website.

7. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming sitemap submission guarantees indexing.
  • Requesting indexing repeatedly without investigating the issue.
  • Confusing impressions with clicks.
  • Assuming every non-indexed page is a technical error.
  • Making major SEO changes based on a single day's data.
  • Ignoring the search queries and pages that already perform well.

8. Practice Task

For practice, imagine you manage a website and answer these questions:

  1. What is Google Search Console?
  2. What is the difference between clicks and impressions?
  3. How do you calculate CTR?
  4. What is the purpose of URL Inspection?
  5. Does submitting a sitemap guarantee indexing?
  6. What is the difference between Google Search Console and Google Analytics?

Lesson Summary

  • Google Search Console helps monitor Google Search performance and indexing.
  • The Performance report shows clicks, impressions, CTR, and average position.
  • The URL Inspection tool helps investigate individual URLs.
  • A sitemap helps Google discover URLs but does not guarantee indexing.
  • Use actual data to identify SEO improvements.
