LESSON 20

Backlinks & Link Building

Bro, imagine another website publishes an article about website development and includes a link to your website as a useful resource. That link can help visitors discover your website and may help search engines understand its reputation and relevance.

1. What Is a Backlink?

A backlink is a link from one website to another website.

Simple definition: If another website links to your website, you have received a backlink from that website.

Friend explanation

Bro, suppose a popular technology blog recommends your website to its readers. Readers can click that link and visit your website. Search engines may also consider that link when evaluating your website.

2. What Is Link Building?

Link building is the process of earning relevant backlinks from other websites to improve discovery, reputation, and potentially search performance.

Common approaches include:

  • Creating useful content that people naturally want to reference.
  • Publishing original research, guides, or helpful resources.
  • Building genuine relationships with people in your industry.
  • Getting your business listed accurately in relevant directories.
  • Reclaiming legitimate mentions of your brand that lack a link.

3. Why Do Backlinks Matter?

  • They can help users discover your website.
  • They can bring referral traffic from other websites.
  • They may help search engines discover pages.
  • Relevant, editorially earned links can contribute to search ranking signals.
  • They can build awareness and credibility within your industry.

Important: Backlinks do not guarantee higher rankings. Their relevance, context, and how they were obtained matter. Search engines use many signals to determine rankings.

4. What Makes a Backlink Valuable?

Relevance

A link from a website covering a related topic may be more useful than a link from an unrelated website.

Editorial placement

A link that a publisher includes naturally because your content is useful is generally more meaningful than a link placed solely to manipulate search rankings.

Useful referral traffic

A link that brings genuinely interested visitors can provide value even when those visitors do not arrive through a search engine.

Trust and context

Consider whether the linking website is legitimate, whether the surrounding content makes sense, and whether the link helps readers.

5. Follow and Nofollow Links

Links can include attributes that provide search engines with information about the relationship between the linking page and the linked page.

Regular link

<a href="https://your-own-domain.com/">
  Visit the website
</a>

Nofollow link

<a href="https://your-own-domain.com/"
   rel="nofollow">
  Visit the website
</a>

In these examples, replace the domain with your own actual website address before using the code.

The nofollow value indicates that the publisher does not want to associate with the destination in the usual way or endorse it as a regular editorial link. Search engines may treat it as a hint.

Other useful values include:

  • rel="sponsored" — identifies links created as part of advertisements, sponsorships, or similar arrangements.
  • rel="ugc" — identifies links in user-generated content, such as comments or forum posts.

6. Internal Links vs. Backlinks

  • Internal link: Connects one page on your website to another page on the same website.
  • Backlink: Comes from a different website and points to a page on your website.

Your SEO learning portal uses internal links to connect its lessons. For example, you can revisit the Internal Linking lesson to understand how internal links help users navigate a website.

7. Safe Link-Building Strategies

Create Link-Worthy Content

Publish original research, detailed tutorials, useful tools, or resources that genuinely help your audience.

Build Real Industry Relationships

Connect with relevant publishers, developers, and business owners. Share useful information instead of asking for links without offering any value.

Use Relevant Business Directories

If you run a real business, maintain accurate information in legitimate directories that are useful to your customers.

Promote Your Content

Share useful articles with people who may genuinely benefit from them. A publisher might reference your content if it provides something valuable to their readers.

8. Link-Building Practices to Avoid

  • Buying links to manipulate search rankings.
  • Participating in link schemes or link farms.
  • Creating large numbers of low-quality directory links.
  • Automatically generating links through spam comments.
  • Excessively exchanging links only to manipulate rankings.
  • Publishing irrelevant guest articles purely for backlinks.
  • Using automated tools to create unnatural links at scale.

These practices can violate search engine spam policies and may lead to ranking problems or other search-related consequences.

9. What Is Anchor Text?

Anchor text is the visible, clickable text of a hyperlink.

<a href="internal-linking.html">
  Learn about internal linking
</a>

In this example, Learn about internal linking is the anchor text.

Use clear, descriptive anchor text that tells readers what to expect when they follow a link. Avoid forcing the same keyword into every link.

10. How to Evaluate Your Backlinks

When reviewing backlinks, consider these questions:

  1. Does the linking website cover a relevant topic?
  2. Does the link appear naturally in useful content?
  3. Could the link bring interested visitors?
  4. Was the link obtained without manipulative practices?
  5. Does the linking page provide context for the destination?

Don't judge a backlink only by a third-party domain authority score. Such scores are estimates created by SEO tools, not official Google ranking metrics.

11. Practical Task

Bro, imagine you have created a detailed guide about SEO for beginners. Think about how you could earn relevant backlinks naturally.

  1. Identify websites that publish content about SEO or web development.
  2. Think of an original resource that would help their readers.
  3. Prepare a short, personalized message explaining why your resource is useful.
  4. Review any backlinks you earn for relevance and quality.
  5. Track referral visits and relevant improvements over time.

12. Quick Revision

  • A backlink is a link from another website to yours.
  • Link building focuses on earning useful, relevant links.
  • Relevance and editorial context matter.
  • Anchor text is the visible clickable text.
  • Internal links connect pages within the same website.
  • Avoid link schemes and manipulative link-building practices.
  • Backlinks can help SEO, but they do not guarantee rankings.
